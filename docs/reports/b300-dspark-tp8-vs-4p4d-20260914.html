DeepSeek-V4-Flash DSpark on B300: Single-Instance TP8 vs PD 4P4D

A concurrency-sweep latency and throughput study across two inference stacks and two GPU topologies on a single p6-b300.48xlarge (8× NVIDIA B300). The goal: understand where prefill/decode disaggregation (4P4D) helps TPOT and inter-token latency, and where it does not.

Background & objective

You asked us to characterize DeepSeek-V4-Flash with DSpark speculative decoding on B300 and to determine whether prefill/decode disaggregation (a "4P4D" split: 4 GPUs prefill + 4 GPUs decode) improves time-per-output-token (TPOT) and inter-token latency (ITL) relative to running the whole 8-card machine as a single tensor-parallel engine (TP8). We measured this on two independent serving stacks, each on its own weight quantization, using the same benchmark protocol: a fixed 40,000-token input, 1,500-token output, driven at concurrency 1, 2, 4, 8, and 16, over loopback, with 0 failed requests at every level.

The headline: the two stacks give opposite answers

Group A (vLLM, NVFP4 weights): 4P4D did not lower TPOT. Median TPOT was flat-to-slightly-worse and peak throughput dropped 15–30%. The one durable PD win was a calmer ITL p99 tail at c8 (224.70→153.29 ms).

Group B (SGLang, preview mxfp4 weights): 4P4D won decisively on TPOT and the ITL p99 tail. PD TPOT median stayed flat at ~1.3 ms across all concurrency while TP8 climbed to 15.02 ms at c16; PD's ITL p99 never exceeded ~11 ms while TP8's exploded to 507.52 ms at c16. The cost was much higher TTFT.

Why opposite? DSpark speculative acceptance differed sharply between the two stacks. In the SGLang preview run, DSpark accept length was 5.47–5.76 (strong acceptance), so dedicating 4 cards purely to decode kept per-token latency extremely low and flat. In the vLLM NVFP4 run, accept length was only 1.78–2.48, so halving decode parallelism to 4 cards cost more than prefill isolation saved.

Group A — vLLM NVFP4 DSpark: TP8 vs PD 4P4D

vLLM nightly (0.29.1rc1.dev9) · nvidia/DeepSeek-V4-Flash-nvfp4-DSpark (NVFP4) · --kv-cache-dtype fp8

Round 1 ran the whole machine as a single TP8 engine, direct to the engine. Round 2 split the same stack and the same weights into a prefill instance (GPUs 0–3, TP4, kv_producer) and a decode instance (GPUs 4–7, TP4, kv_consumer) over the NixlConnector, benched through the disagg proxy. DSpark stayed active on both sides. This group targeted lower TPOT.

Charts plot the values reported in the two vLLM run summaries. TPOT/ITL/TTFT are median unless the series name says p99. vLLM's default bench saved median + p99 only (no p90) for Group A.4

Group A insight

Splitting to 4 decode cards removed prefill interference but halved decode parallelism. With DSpark acceptance only 1.78–2.48, the decode-parallelism loss dominated: TPOT median went 3.17→3.09 ms at c1 (marginal), then ticked up at c2/c4/c8/c16 (e.g. c8 6.00→6.61, c16 8.71→8.99 ms). The single clear PD benefit was the c8 ITL p99 tail (224.70→153.29 ms, ~32% lower), bought with a 15–30% peak-throughput sacrifice. For a pure-TPOT objective on this stack, TP8 remains the better configuration.

Group B — SGLang preview DSpark (mxfp4): TP8 vs PD 4P4D

SGLang v0.5.17-cu130 · preview deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4-Flash-DSpark (mxfp4) · flashinfer_mxfp4 MoE

Rounds 3 and 4 ran the preview DSpark weights on the same 8× B300, first as a whole-machine TP8 engine, then as an SGLang-native 4P4D split (prefill TP4 GPUs 0–3 + decode TP4 GPUs 4–7, nixl transfer backend, DSpark on the decode side, benched through the SGLang PD router). Same 40k/1.5k protocol.

SGLang bench captured median, p90, and p99. The PD ITL median (~7.1 ms flat) is a per-DSpark-step interval through the router, not a per-token gap; PD's true per-token cadence equals its TPOT (~1.3 ms).3

Group B insight

Dedicating 4 cards purely to decode kept PD TPOT median flat at ~1.3 ms across all concurrency (1.22→1.32 ms from c1 to c16), with TPOT p99 ≤2.12 ms everywhere. TP8's TPOT rose steeply to 15.02 ms median (37.13 ms p99) at c16, and its ITL p99 tail blew out to 507.52 ms at c16 from prefill/decode contention on the shared cards. PD held ITL p99 ≤10.96 ms at every level. This is the reverse of Group A, driven by DSpark accept length 5.47–5.76 (strong acceptance): with ~5.5 tokens accepted per decode step, 4 decode cards were ample and prefill isolation was pure upside for TPOT. The cost is TTFT: PD c16 median ~21.9 s vs TP8 ~4.2 s, because prefill has only 4 cards plus the PD handoff.

Core insight: why the two groups disagree

DSpark acceptance is the hinge

The single variable that flips the verdict is speculative-decode acceptance. In Group B (SGLang preview mxfp4), DSpark accepted 5.47–5.76 tokens per step — each decode step emits ~5.5 tokens, so 4 dedicated decode cards produce very low, flat per-token latency and prefill isolation is pure upside. In Group A (vLLM NVFP4), DSpark accepted only 1.78–2.48 tokens per step, so decode is far more compute-bound; cutting decode from 8 to 4 cards loses parallelism that outweighs the prefill-isolation benefit, and TPOT does not improve.

Practically: PD 4P4D is a TPOT/tail-latency win only when speculative acceptance is high enough that 4 decode cards keep up. Under weak acceptance, the whole-machine TP8 engine is the better TPOT choice. Because Group A and Group B also differ in inference engine and weight quantization, they are two independent comparisons and must not be treated as a single like-for-like 4-way plot.2

AWS-recommended next steps

  1. Choose topology by target concurrency and latency objective. If your production SLO is low, stable TPOT and a controlled inter-token tail, and you are on the SGLang preview DSpark stack, PD 4P4D is the clear pick: it held TPOT median at ~1.3 ms and ITL p99 ≤10.96 ms all the way to c16, versus TP8's 15.02 ms TPOT and 507.52 ms ITL p99 at c16. If prompt responsiveness (TTFT) dominates, or you are on the vLLM NVFP4 stack, prefer whole-machine TP8.
  2. Treat PD disaggregation as a TPOT lever for speculative-decode-heavy workloads. The preview DSpark stack's strong acceptance (5.47–5.76) is exactly the regime where separating decode onto dedicated cards pays off. For TPOT-sensitive products on that stack, PD 4P4D is worth the higher TTFT and the ~4-card prefill limit.
  3. Mind the TTFT trade in PD. The PD topology raised Group B c16 TTFT median to ~21.9 s vs ~4.2 s on TP8. For interactive prompt-heavy use, either cap concurrency, add prefill capacity, or keep TP8 where first-token latency is the KPI.
  4. Validate DSpark acceptance directly on the PD path before committing production capacity. Accept length was only observable on the direct-to-engine TP8 runs (5.47–5.76 for SGLang preview, 1.78–2.48 for vLLM NVFP4); it is not surfaced through either the vLLM disagg proxy or the SGLang PD router.3 We recommend instrumenting the decode engine to confirm realized acceptance under production traffic, since the TPOT verdict hinges on it.
  5. Re-benchmark with your production prompt mix and output lengths. These runs used a fixed 40k input / 1.5k output at concurrency 1–16. Shorter prompts, longer generations, or bursty concurrency will shift the prefill/decode balance and therefore the TP8-vs-PD crossover point. A short confirmation sweep on representative traffic is the safest way to lock in a topology.
